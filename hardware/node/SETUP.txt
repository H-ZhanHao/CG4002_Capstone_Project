HOW THE CONNECTIONS WORK

Laptop <-> ExerciseHip: USB or Bluetooth commands and hip telemetry.
Laptop <-> ExerciseWrist: USB or Bluetooth commands and wrist telemetry.
Hip <-> Wrist: ESP-NOW activation handover, mode synchronization and WRIST rep feedback.

Each monitor shows only its own board. Counts and exercise selection are not

must acknowledge standby. Bluetooth pairing alone does not enable rep reception.

LOCAL SENSOR HISTORY (STEP 2, BEFORE COMMS INTEGRATION)

Both HIP and WRIST continuously retain successful MPU6050 reads, including while
their counters are in standby or waiting for activation. Each sample contains
local uptime in milliseconds (timestamped at read start), acceleration XYZ in
m/s2 including gravity, and gyro XYZ in rad/s. Values are saved before exercise
filtering or gyro correction. Failed reads do not insert zero or stale samples.

SensorHistoryConfig in SensorHistory.h sets a 3000 ms window and 7000 ms retention.
At the configured 100 Hz this uses 701 ring slots, about 19.6 KB. The manual window
command inspects metadata directly without allocating a full sample snapshot.
The history array is static, off the main task stack. Data is in RAM only and is
lost on reboot. ACTIVATE, STANDBY, RESET, exercise changes and mode changes preserve
this history and the sampling cadence. Generic peak detection is described below;
automatic window transmission is not implemented yet.

Bench inspection, on each board's USB or Bluetooth serial monitor:
1. Send BUFFER after boot. It reports the node, stored sample count, oldest/newest
   timestamps, covered span and newest sample age (all times in milliseconds).
2. Send WINDOW before three seconds of samples are available. Expect EMPTY or
   INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY. No samples are padded or invented.
3. After at least three seconds of successful sampling, send WINDOW again. It
   inspects [latest sample time - 3000, latest sample time] and prints a summary.
   READY means enough history with no gap greater than 20 ms. SAMPLE_GAP means
   the actual samples are present, but at least one interval/edge gap exceeds
   20 ms. max_gap_ms reports the largest gap; smaller jitter is not resampled.
   At exactly 100 Hz with both endpoints included, there are 301 samples. With
   real timing jitter or a t0 between samples, counts/endpoints can differ.
4. To simulate a delayed trigger, note newest_ms from BUFFER and send
   WINDOW:<that timestamp>, for example WINDOW:12000. This selects [9000,12000]
   regardless of when the command arrives, provided those times are retained.
   At nominal cadence, the extra history allows up to four seconds of delay,
   shared between peak confirmation and any later trigger/transport handling.
   Peak confirmation is capped at 2500 ms, leaving at least 1500 ms for delivery
   at that limit. This is a budget, not a guarantee of real transport latency.
   END_NOT_AVAILABLE means t0 is newer than the last successful sample.
   INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY means the requested history is unavailable/expired.
5. RESET or change the exercise, then repeat WINDOW. The history should remain.
   Repeat in standby on each board; no peer connection is required for buffering.

WINDOW prints metadata only, avoiding a large serial dump that could delay the
sampling loop. It does not start counting, send ESP-NOW triggers, or send samples
to Ultra96. WINDOW without a timestamp anchors to the latest successful sample;
check BUFFER's age_ms to spot stale data after sensor failures.

For later integration, ExerciseNode::copySensorWindow(t0, destination, capacity)
copies samples in chronological order. Call it from the same main loop as update().
The caller owns the copy; subsequent ring writes cannot change it. OUTPUT_TOO_SMALL
reports the required count without writing a partial result. The local scheduler
below uses this same extraction behaviour. Clock estimation and coordinated-start
logic are available below; their actual comms transport is not connected yet.
Timestamps are local to each board and wrap modulo 2^32 milliseconds; comparisons
assume timestamps less than 2^31 ms apart. Reboot starts a new local time reference.

GENERIC MOVEMENT PEAKS (STEP 3, BEFORE COMMS INTEGRATION)

Both nodes detect movement independently, including standby, failed activation
and active counting. No exercise selection or standstill calibration is needed.
The detector does not recognize an exercise, validate a rep, activate counting,
change LEDs, send peer trigger packets or transmit sensor windows to Ultra96.
Walking, handling the board and other non-exercise motion may also trigger it.

GenericPeakDetector.h contains provisional, shared movement thresholds, separate
from ExercisePresets.h. Tune them with recordings from the actual wearer/mounting:
  Acceleration gate: abs(|acceleration XYZ| - 9.81) >= 0.50 m/s2.
  Rotation gate: |gyro XYZ| >= 0.60 rad/s, assuming zero gyro bias.
Either channel can trigger, including rotation with little acceleration change.
A three-sample median filter rejects isolated spikes. The larger of the two
normalized signals must remain above its gate for at least 100 ms. A peak is
confirmed once the score falls at least 25% from its maximum for at least 40 ms.
The filtered peak can lag the raw sensor maximum by a sample; t0 is the timestamp
of the highest filtered score, not the later time at which the decline confirms it.
On a flat maximum, the first sample at that maximum supplies t0.

After a confirmed peak, both filtered signals must drop below 45% of their gates
for 80 ms to rearm. New excursions during the one-second trigger cooldown are
ignored until the signals settle again. This is trigger debounce during motion,
not a three-second startup wait. Sustained motion may produce only one trigger;
an accepted window session continues independently of subsequent peaks.
Candidates older than 2500 ms are discarded. Invalid values or sample gaps over
100 ms discard the partial peak/filter state, without removing the cooldown.
Counter RESET, exercise/mode changes and activation preserve the detector state.

Bench inspection, with either node powered and its serial monitor open:
1. Leave counting in standby. Small sensor noise should not print PEAK events.
2. Move the board through an acceleration or rotation peak. A confirmed movement
   automatically prints metadata such as:
     [PEAK | HIP | t0_ms=8240 | confirmed_ms=8340 | accel=1.60 m/s2 | gyro=0.10 rad/s]
   This is a USB/Bluetooth diagnostic line, not a comms packet or a sensor window.
3. Send WINDOW:8240 using the actual printed t0, promptly enough to retain its
   history. Expect READY when three seconds of healthy samples cover that time.
   The returned end time is t0, not confirmed_ms or the time the command arrived.
4. A peak during the first three seconds is still detected, but WINDOW can report
   INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY. No missing pre-boot samples are invented. Waiting for
   initial history does not require the wearer to stand still.
5. Repeat on WRIST, including rotation resembling jumping-jack arm movement.
   Verify that standby counts remain inactive and peaks cause no LED feedback.
   Try walking too: triggers are possible; exercise recognition belongs to comms.

The stream controller receives each fresh peak directly and requests a shared
session from comms. consumeGenericPeak(event) remains an optional diagnostic
observer: it returns the first unconsumed event
once without consuming a prepared window or resetting detector cooldown. Later
peaks are logged without replacing that pending observation. Reboot clears it.
Do not use this diagnostic observer to drive a second window scheduler.

LOCAL SLIDING WINDOWS (STEP 4, BEFORE COMMS INTEGRATION)

Each node starts ARMED. With Step 5, its first peak requests a coordinated session
and prints TRIGGER/WAIT_COMMS. A peak alone no longer creates local windows.
After an accepted JOIN, sequence 0 is copied immediately using the supplied local
t0: [t0 - 3000, t0]. Following endpoints are t0 + 1000, t0 + 2000, etc.
Each window is still three seconds long. Later peaks do not restart the timeline.
No further peaks are needed for windows to continue. Both nodes use the same
coordinator/session ID and translated local timestamps. STREAM:START remains an
explicit local bench override (coordinator=0), without the comms handshake.

Windows use actual samples, including both endpoints when present. At exactly
100 Hz that is 301 samples; no padding, interpolation or fixed-size resampling is
performed. If t0 falls between samples, each copied sample stays within the
requested interval. Endpoint times remain on the t0-based grid despite loop jitter.
The scheduler waits up to 100 ms for a successful read to cover a due endpoint;
after that it records END_NOT_AVAILABLE/EMPTY rather than waiting indefinitely.
It runs even when reads fail or the main loop is between scheduled sensor reads.

An early peak still requests its original t0. After JOIN, its first windows may
report EMPTY/INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY with no sample payload. Later windows become
usable once enough history exists, without requiring a new peak. SAMPLE_GAP
includes real samples with a gap flag. Consumers must check each window's status.
If the loop stalls, at most the three most recent due slots are caught up, one
per update; older slots increment skipped. Sequence numbers retain their original
positions, so missing windows remain visible. The cadence never shifts to now.

There is a FIFO queue of three snapshots (about 25.5 KB), in addition to the 19.6 KB
history ring. Diagnostics need no full-window scratch. All large arrays are static.
Each snapshot holds 302 samples at the configured 100 Hz: 301 inclusive samples
plus one for boundary timing jitter. If an interval exceeds that capacity, its
record reports OUTPUT_TOO_SMALL with no payload; samples are never truncated.
New ring samples cannot change queued/copied snapshots. A full queue preserves
its existing windows, including sequence 0, and drops each new due window with
QUEUE_FULL and an incremented dropped counter. Sampling/counting continue.
There is no comms consumer yet, so unattended testing fills the queue after three
records. STREAM:NEXT removes one record for bench inspection; it prints metadata
only. Future comms must consume the queue regularly. This is not lossless storage.
USB diagnostics have a 2 KB transmit buffer to reduce normal logging delays;
excessive output can still block if that buffer fills.

Commands (USB or Bluetooth, one command per line):
  STREAM                  Show ARMED/RUNNING/STOPPED, session/t0, queue and loss counts.
  STREAM:STOP             Stop preparation, clear the queue and pending request,
                          invalidate the old arm ticket, disable peak requests.
  STREAM:ARM              Allow a new peak request or peer-initiated JOIN if stopped.
                          A running session keeps its anchor. No detector reset.
  STREAM:START            Start now using the latest sample timestamp, without a peak.
  STREAM:START:<t0_ms>    Start from a supplied past/current local timestamp. A future
                          t0 or an already-running session is rejected unchanged.
  STREAM:NEXT             Copy/remove the oldest queued record and print its summary.
                          This is a diagnostic consumer, not transmission to Ultra96.

There is no automatic inactivity timeout yet: preparation continues until STOP.
Counter ACTIVATE, STANDBY, RESET, exercise/mode changes and peer LED events do not
stop or restart the window session. STREAM controls do not activate/stop counting.
STOP preserves sensor history. ARM requires a new peak request or a new coordinated
JOIN, never a previous pending request. Reboot clears everything and creates a new
boot ID. Local START numbers are per node; coordinated IDs come from comms.

Typical output (example values, exact cadence assumed):
  [PEAK | HIP | t0_ms=8240 | confirmed_ms=8340 | accel=1.60 m/s2 | gyro=0.10 rad/s]
  [TRIGGER | HIP | boot=42 | arm=1 | t0_ms=8240 | confirmed_ms=8340 | WAIT_COMMS]
No FRAME appears until comms accepts a start. For example, after:
  STREAM:JOIN:42:1:99:12:8240
  [JOIN | HIP | STARTED | coord=99 | session=12 | boot=42 | arm=1 | t0_ms=8240]
  [FRAME | HIP | coord=99 | session=12 | seq=0 | READY | start_ms=5240 | end_ms=8240 | samples=301 | queue=1 | drop=0 | skip=0]
  [FRAME | HIP | coord=99 | session=12 | seq=1 | READY | start_ms=6240 | end_ms=9240 | samples=301 | queue=2 | drop=0 | skip=0]
FRAME means prepared locally, never sent. FRAME TAKEN means removed by STREAM:NEXT.
If the queue fills, expect QUEUE_FULL on following intervals until records are taken.

Bench check on each board independently:
1. Power the node and leave counting in standby. STREAM should report ARMED.
2. Allow at least three seconds of samples, then move through a peak. Expect
   TRIGGER/WAIT_COMMS and no FRAME. Promptly send JOIN with the printed boot/arm/t0
   and nonzero coordinator/session numbers. Expect seq=0, then one attempt/second.
3. STREAM:NEXT displays/removes the oldest snapshot. Repeat about once per second
   to emulate a consumer; otherwise observe queue=3 followed by QUEUE_FULL.
4. Repeat the identical JOIN: expect ALREADY_RUNNING, without a new sequence 0.
   STREAM:STOP clears the queue and pending request. After STREAM:ARM, an old JOIN
   must report STALE_TARGET; it must not start windows again.
5. Test STREAM:START without movement. Stop before trying another anchor/session.
6. Counter reset/exercise changes should preserve the current t0 and sequence.

Integration hooks in ExerciseNode.h (same main loop as update(), never a radio callback):
  startWindowStream(t0) [local bench override] / stopWindowStream() / armWindowStream()
  windowStreamStatus()
  takeSensorWindow(destination)
Allocate the ScheduledWindow destination statically/on the heap on ESP32, not on
the main task stack. takeSensorWindow returns false without changing the output
when empty. On true it copies and removes exactly one record; the caller owns it.
Only samples[0 .. info.window.count) is valid, and only if window.copied() is true.
SAMPLE_GAP still needs the consumer's policy; unavailable-history records have no
sample payload. Taking is not an acknowledgement of delivery; a future transport
must retain its own copy if it needs retries. The manual NEXT command consumes
the same queue, so do not use it alongside the real comms consumer.

COORDINATED WINDOW SESSIONS (STEP 5)

StreamCoordination.h/.cpp contains NodeStreamControl (used by both firmware
targets) and StreamCoordinator (portable comms-side logic, not instantiated on
either node). The two-node host test runs this coordinator with both node controls,
different clocks and independent sensor histories. Actual comms transport is still
pending: no network port is opened and no IMU payload is transmitted in this step.
USB/Bluetooth commands can exercise the receiver now; logs are bench diagnostics,
not a finalized network wire format. ESP-NOW activation/LED packets are unchanged.

Lifecycle and ownership:
1. Comms owns ONE StreamCoordinator and serializes its calls. Call begin() with a
   fresh, nonzero random coordinator ID at startup. Session numbers increment
   within that coordinator boot; the pair (coordinator, session) identifies a set.
2. Probe both armed nodes' clocks. Both need a fresh accepted clock measurement
   before a trigger can start a set. Readiness here means armed/control readiness,
   not guaranteed three-second sensor coverage; each window reports data quality.
3. The first accepted trigger chooses t0. If both trigger together, the second
   receives EXISTING and cannot change t0. There is no main streamer: both get a
   targeted START and keep their own three-second windows / separate data pipeline.
4. Comms polls START for each role and delivers it until acknowledged. A node
   accepts its own boot/arm ticket, including when it has detected no local peak.
   Identical retries return ALREADY_RUNNING without clearing a queue, resetting
   sequence numbers or replaying sequence zero. Changed t0 under the same session
   returns CONFLICT; a different session while running returns BUSY.
5. Both acknowledgements make bothStarted() true. This means both accepted the
   control session; it does not guarantee sample availability, successful payload
   delivery or continued connectivity. Comms still checks every window status.
6. An explicit END stops that matching session. STOP also cancels an unaccepted
   request. Stop both nodes, close() the coordinator session, then ARM both for
   another set. A partial-start abort must also STOP any node that never accepted
   START, since it cannot recognize that session's END. Re-probe after rearming.
   A reboot/changed arm during an active session requires explicit close/recovery;
   the coordinator never silently substitutes a different node boot.

The node's first trigger is retained until JOIN or STOP. pollStreamTrigger() offers
the same request every 500 ms; consuming the optional generic peak diagnostic has
no effect. The coordinator's pollStart(role, now, start) likewise retries every
500 ms until a matching successful acknowledgement. A transport must service these
APIs; printing a TRIGGER line alone does not send it to a central coordinator.
There is no automatic inactivity timeout, stop, failover or fallback local start.
The adapter must explicitly stop/cancel on its chosen abort policy.

Clock measurement and translation:
- Comms records c1 when sending a probe, and c4 when its correlated reply arrives.
  The node returns n2 (command receive time), n3 (reply preparation time), its
  role/boot/arm ticket and whether it is armed. Match the probe ID and current
  transport connection before calling observeClock(reply, c1, c4).
- The offset estimate is midpoint(n2,n3) - midpoint(c1,c4), modulo uint32.
  Processing time is removed from the network round-trip estimate. Measurements
  with network RTT > 40 ms or impossible timestamp ordering are rejected; mappings
  expire after 30 seconds. Those limits live in StreamCoordinationConfig.
- Timing asymmetry remains uncertainty (roughly half the network RTT, plus ms
  rounding). clockUncertaintyMs() exposes it. This is not exact synchronization;
  oscillator drift and real link timing still need hardware validation. Refresh
  measurements while armed. A session freezes its chosen local anchors; later
  measurements do not move a running window grid. Long-session drift correction
  and any classifier-required alignment tolerance must be agreed during integration.
- coordinator_t0 = source_peak_ms - source_offset;
  target_local_t0 = coordinator_t0 + target_offset.
  Thus different printed t0 numbers may describe the same physical instant.
- Node clocks continue unchanged; no clock is reset. All time arithmetic assumes
  intervals less than 2^31 ms. The seven-second ring allows about four seconds
  for peak confirmation PLUS coordination/delivery delay. A late JOIN reports
  INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY rather than inventing/reanchoring the first window; subsequent
  windows can recover. A coordinator must handle these data-quality failures.

Additional USB/Bluetooth bench commands (newline terminated):
  STREAM:REQUEST
    Show the pending trigger without consuming it, or TRIGGER/NONE.
  STREAM:CLOCK:<probe>
    Reply CLOCK with probe, boot, arm, rx_ms, tx_ms, armed. Use automated timestamped
    probes for actual clock estimation; manually typing commands cannot measure RTT.
  STREAM:JOIN:<boot>:<arm>:<coordinator>:<session>:<local_t0>
    Target this node's CURRENT boot/arm (shown by STREAM/CLOCK/TRIGGER). Coordinator
    and session must be nonzero uint32 values. A future local_t0 is INVALID.
    Both boards receive the same coordinator/session, but their own ticket and
    translated local_t0. A quiet board accepts JOIN while armed.
  STREAM:END:<coordinator>:<session>
    Stop only the accepted matching session, leave counting/history unchanged.
    An old END cannot stop a newer session. Retries after rearm do not cancel the
    new pending trigger. STREAM:STOP remains the deliberate local override.

Typed node APIs for the future comms adapter (same main loop as update):
  streamIdentity()                         Current role/boot/arm ticket.
  streamClockReply(probe, receivedMs)       Pair a captured local receive time with send time.
  pollStreamTrigger(trigger)               Retained request, with bounded retry cadence.
  joinWindowStream(start)                  Returns a StreamJoinResult for the ACK.
  endWindowStream(coordinator, session)    Scoped stop; false means wrong session.
  takeSensorWindow(destination)            Independent payload consumer for this node.
Capture/queue inbound messages in transport callbacks; apply state changes in the
main loop. Do not call these APIs concurrently. Do not raw-memcpy C++ structs as a
wire format: specify fields, version, units and byte order in the comms adapter.

DEACTIVATE/STANDBY still stop counting only. They leave shared-session requests,
sensor buffering and active window preparation running for classification. ACTIVATE
still requires the existing ESP-NOW standby acknowledgement. Shared START never
activates counting or chooses an exercise; comms routes that feedback separately.

STEP-BY-STEP COMMS REP SESSION

See COMMS_REPS.txt for the full contract and expected output.
1. Select EXERCISE:SQUAT on HIP (or JUMPING_JACK on WRIST).
2. Send ACTIVATE and wait for peer acknowledgement.
3. Send REPS and copy the printed boot/session ticket.
4. Send REPS:<boot>:<session>:1 using those actual numbers. Expect ACCEPTED,
   Reps: 1 on USB/Bluetooth telemetry. LEDs keep their ACTIVITY state.
5. Repeat the message: expect DUPLICATE with no additional count.
6. Move the sensors: rep totals remain unchanged until comms sends a higher total.
7. Send RESET: count becomes zero and a new ticket is printed. Old results are
   rejected. Send a result with the new ticket to begin the next set.

COMMAND REFERENCE (USB OR BLUETOOTH)

ACTIVATE
  Ask the peer to enter standby. After its ACK, accept comms counts with a fresh
  ticket and zero total. Repeating ACTIVATE while active preserves the set.
STANDBY (DEACTIVATE is an alias)
  Stop accepting rep results and cancel pending activation. Sampling, history,
  generic peaks, window preparation and HIP fall monitoring continue.
RESET
  Clear reps and issue a new ticket, preserving active/standby state. Cancel
  pending activation. Does not reset sensor history or the window stream.
REPS / REPS:<boot>:<session>:<total>
  Inspect the current ticket/count or submit a cumulative AR total from comms.
  See COMMS_REPS.txt for result codes, retry rules and lifecycle details.
EXERCISE:<name>
  Select a permitted exercise on this board; plural/spaces/hyphens are accepted.
  A changed exercise applies its default mode, clears reps and changes the ticket.
  Standby stays standby. Selecting the same exercise preserves the set/override.
EXERCISES
  List this board's exercises and indicate those assigned to the other board.
MODE:PAUSE / MODE:CONTINUOUS (PAUSE / CONTINUOUS also accepted)
  Metadata/synchronization retained for compatibility; neither enables local
  counting. Local overrides require activation. An actual change clears reps,
  changes the ticket and syncs to the peer. Repeating the same mode only retries
  sync. Exercise preferences survive handovers until a different exercise or boot.
ACTIVITY / ACTIVITY:<boot>:<sequence>:IDLE|ACTIVE
  Query or update user activity on this board, independently of activation and
  exercise. Use the actual boot ID and an increasing sequence; retries must be
  identical. State persists across rep resets and changes only through comms or
  reboot. See COMMS_REPS.txt. IDLE turns the LED OFF; ACTIVE turns it steadily ON.
STATUS / HELP
  Show activation, user activity, exercise/mode, comms ticket/count and stream/radio status,
  or the command list. Radio readiness does not prove the peer is online.
BUFFER / WINDOW / WINDOW:<t0_ms>
  Inspect sensor history. See LOCAL SENSOR HISTORY above.
STREAM / STREAM:START[:t0_ms] / STREAM:STOP / STREAM:ARM / STREAM:NEXT
  Control local window preparation independently of rep reception.
STREAM:REQUEST / STREAM:CLOCK:<probe> / STREAM:JOIN:<boot>:<arm>:<coordinator>:<session>:<local_t0>
STREAM:END:<coordinator>:<session>
  See COORDINATED WINDOW SESSIONS. These session IDs are separate from REPS tickets.

RETAINED LOCAL DETECTOR PRESETS (BENCH ONLY)

The following describes LocalRepCounting and its two detector algorithms, which
are no longer run or linked into firmware. Editing these limits does not change
AR/comms counts. ExercisePresets.h still supplies board/mode selection metadata.

Pause mode: acceleration-magnitude deviation from nominal gravity (9.81 m/s2).
  Exercise       Board   Start (m/s2)  End (m/s2)  Peak (m/s2)  Time (ms)
  PUSHUP         HIP     0.50          0.20        0.70         700-6000
  SITUP          WRIST   0.50          0.20        0.70         700-6000
  SQUAT          HIP     0.80          0.30        1.00         700-6000
  LUNGE          HIP     0.80          0.30        1.00         700-6000
  LEG_RAISE      HIP     0.50          0.20        0.70         700-6000
  JUMPING_JACK   WRIST   0.50          0.20        0.70         700-6000

Squat/lunge reuse historical squat calibration gates as counting limits.
Push-up uses provisional old chest settings at the new hip placement. Sit-up,
leg-raise and jumping-jack values are mocks. These are not measured final profiles.
Pause durations include the finishing pause. This detector counts motion bursts;
compare its output against full physical reps while tuning.

Continuous mode has separately editable MOCK gyro limits for every exercise:
start 0.60 rad/s, end 0.18 rad/s, peak 0.70 rad/s, minimum angular travel 0.60 rad
in EACH direction, full-cycle duration 600-6000 ms. Axis and initial direction
are discovered each session. One-second axis windows may take longer with weak
or mixed motion. Acceleration thresholds cannot substitute for gyro limits.

LocalRepCounting::begin uses fixed sensor references:
GeneralConfig::GRAVITY_MPS2 = 9.81 m/s2 and GYRO_BIAS_RAD_S = {0, 0, 0} rad/s.
No new sensor baseline or gyro offset is measured. These are nominal assumptions,
separate from exercise thresholds. Actual sensor offsets can cause missed/extra
counts; test with the wearer/mounting before relying on these defaults.
LocalRepCounting starts from zero after begin(); earlier movement is not replayed.
Continuous mode still discovers its axis/direction from movement each session.
Wrist sit-ups need consistent arm position relative to the torso.

ACTIVITY LED AND RADIO BEHAVIOR

Each board's runtime LED follows its accepted ACTIVITY state: OFF for IDLE,
steadily ON for ACTIVE, independent of exercise or counting activation. Send
ACTIVITY separately to both boards with their own boot IDs; it is not forwarded
over ESP-NOW. There are no ready/rep flashes, and legacy peer LED packets
cannot override activity. Reset, mode/exercise changes, standby and disconnects
preserve activity. Only an explicit IDLE or reboot clears ACTIVE.
The fatal MPU6050 startup-error blink remains as a wiring diagnostic.

ESP-NOW retains the 24-byte version-4 protocol; its legacy LED pattern support
is unused by the current GPIO path. New REP_FEEDBACK/ACK event kinds provide
WRIST-to-HIP buzzer feedback with deduplication and bounded retries (250 ms,
5 attempts, 1.25-second queue age, at most 8 pending events). See COMMS_REPS.txt
for diagnostics, cancellation, protocol fields and a two-board bench test.
There are no sensor
streams, forwarded counts, remote fall messages, discovery broadcasts or heartbeats.
Activation and mode sync try five times at 500 ms intervals (2.5-second deadline).
No activation ACK means activation fails. If every ACK is lost, both may remain
in standby. HIP wins simultaneous pending requests. A delayed deactivation may
conservatively stop a board but cannot start it.

Once active, a board accepts comms counts even if the peer loses power/radio reception.
After a board reboots, resend ACTIVITY with its new boot ID to restore its LED.
ACTIVATE on an already-active board does not repeat the handshake.
After either board reboots, resend the desired MODE from the active board to sync.
After HIP reboots, use STANDBY then ACTIVATE on WRIST to refresh the feedback
handshake and obtain the new WRIST REPS ticket. Upload both boards for feedback.
Both Bluetooth and ESP-NOW share the ESP32 radio; test switching on real boards.

BENCH AND WEARABLE CHECKS

- Follow COMMS_REPS.txt on both roles using USB first, then actual Bluetooth comms.
- Verify that motion alone never changes reps or generates rep LED events.
- Check duplicates, old tickets after RESET, standby rejection, and reconnect.
- Verify handover, exercise restrictions and mode synchronization on both boards.
- Verify BUFFER/WINDOW, generic peaks and coordinated window sessions still work
  while rep reception is active or in standby.
- Verify HIP rep beeps, fall-alarm priority and steady activity LEDs on real boards.
- Verify WRIST rep increases beep on standby HIP, retries don't replay sounds,
  and WRIST RESET cancels pending feedback without changing HIP's own count.
- Host tests validate logic; real AR/comms delivery requires a live integration test.
